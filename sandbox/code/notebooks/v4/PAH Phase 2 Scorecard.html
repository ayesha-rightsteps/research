  • Same shape. In both, the method that re-assigns targets dynamically beats MAPPO with a fixed assignment (paper: DA-MAPPO 90 vs MAPPO 64; ours: DA-MAPPO (2D) beats MAPPO by +7.0 [2.3, 11.6] at Stage 3), and IPPO is the weakest MARL method.
  • Our addition sits on top. MAPPO + PAH is above every base-paper method at both stages, in the same way DA-MAPPO is above its baselines in the paper.
  • Our baselines score lower than the paper's. Our Stage 3 is harder for them: all methods share our small 64-unit network and 8,000-episode budget, with no curriculum.
Where the gap comes from

Splitting the Stage 3 gap to DA-MAPPO (2D)

DA-MAPPO (2D) and Plain MAPPO use the same agent, reward and settings. The only difference is that Plain MAPPO sees conflict-graph features instead of raw teammate positions. That lets us split the 53.8-point gap into three steps, each measured on its own.

All three steps help at both stages. PAH's own share is the smallest of the three but its interval stays above zero at both stages.

Limits

What a reviewer may ask

  • One 2D environment. No claim is made against the paper's 3D Gazebo numbers.
  • Shared hyperparameters. Every method used the same settings, as the base paper did. The base-paper methods were not tuned separately.
  • PAH vs hand-coded τ-rule at Stage 2 is unresolved: +2.0 [−2.6, 6.6]. PAH is clearly ahead at Stage 3.
  • PAH design. One PAH fix came after looking at the first Stage 3 seed-42 result. It corrected a bias and was not tuned on the test seeds; this is recorded in the Phase 1b README.
  • RMAPPO is missing. Its 20 runs will be trained next and added here.
  • Multiple comparisons. The secondary comparisons in Table 3 are not corrected for multiple testing.
